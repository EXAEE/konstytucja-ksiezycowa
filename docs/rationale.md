# Rationale

## Why draft a Lunar Constitution at all?

International space law (especially the Outer Space Treaty) states what states *may not* do with the Moon (e.g. appropriate it), but it poorly regulates everyday life, work, conflict, and exploitation *on site* once permanent bases and multiple actors appear.

This Constitution is meant to fill that gap as a **local layer**: open, limited, person-centred — before rules are set only by facts on the ground, corporate contracts, or unilateral base regulations.

## Why these principles

1. **Prohibition of sovereignty over the Moon as a whole** — consistent with the OST; without it any “constitution” becomes a colonial act.
2. **Dignity of the person above flag and firm** — in an environment where survival depends on systems, technical and economic servitude is an easy failure mode.
3. **Water and means of survival beyond monopoly** — on the Moon this is a matter of life, not only of markets.
4. **Protection of heritage and environment** — regolith, ice, and historic sites are finite on a human scale.
5. **Force only in defence of life** — the Moon should not become a range for territorial claims.
6. **Voluntary adoption** — the project does not pretend to “enact” law for the UN or states; it builds a norm through accession.

## What version 0.1 deliberately does not do

- It does not create a full criminal or tax code
- It does not establish a lunar government with a monopoly on force
- It does not settle every dispute over ownership of extracted resources (that needs further debate and international law)
- It does not claim binding force without adoption by actors operating on the Moon
