# First Lunar Constitution

**Open draft framework for activity on the Moon.**

This is not binding international law.  
It is a constitutional proposal: a text to discuss, amend, and co-create.

The project acknowledges existing frameworks (Outer Space Treaty 1967, Moon Agreement 1979, Artemis Accords and other soft law). It does not claim to override them. It aims to fill a gap: what should apply *on site* when people, bases, and economic activity are actually present on the surface.

# First Lunar Constitution

**Open draft — working version 0.1**

Preamble  
We, persons present and future on the Moon,  
knowing that this celestial body belongs to no state,  
recognizing the dignity of every human being above origin, flag, and corporation,  
wishing to avoid repeating on the Moon the worst patterns from Earth,  
set down these principles as law to apply wherever our actions reach.

---

## Title I — Foundations

**Art. 1.**  
The Moon is a common heritage.  
No one may proclaim state sovereignty over it or exclusive ownership of the celestial body as a whole.

**Art. 2.**  
This Constitution applies to the activities of persons, bases, vehicles, and installations on the lunar surface, subsurface, and in lunar orbits, insofar as it does not conflict with binding international law accepted by the community of states.

**Art. 3.**  
Human dignity is inviolable.  
No local law, base regulation, or private contract may set it aside.

---

## Title II — Persons and Freedoms

**Art. 4.**  
Every person on the Moon has the right to life, safety, medical care within available means, and return to Earth where technically possible and not endangering others.

**Art. 5.**  
Slavery, forced labour, trafficking in persons, and all forms of servitude are prohibited.

**Art. 6.**  
Freedom of thought, conscience, expression, and association is subject only to such limits as are necessary for the safety of life in an environment hostile to life.

**Art. 7.**  
No one may be discriminated against on the basis of earthly origin, citizenship, sex, language, religion, orientation, disability, or property status.

---

## Title III — Safety and the Lunar Environment

**Art. 8.**  
Protection of human life takes priority over profit, mission prestige, and experiment.

**Art. 9.**  
Activity on the Moon shall be conducted with care for the lunar environment: regolith, ice, and landforms of scientific or cultural value are protected from unnecessary destruction.

**Art. 10.**  
Waste, pollution, and abandoned installations may not be left in ways that endanger other missions or future generations of presence.

**Art. 11.**  
Zones of special scientific or historical significance (including sites of first landings) may be recognized as common heritage and excluded from destructive exploitation.

---

## Title IV — Resources and Economic Activity

**Art. 12.**  
Use of lunar resources (water/ice, regolith, elements) is permissible provided that it:
1. does not violate Art. 1,
2. does not unreasonably deny others access,
3. is transparent as to scale and effects,
4. does not needlessly destroy protected objects.

**Art. 13.**  
A monopoly over a key resource essential to survival (e.g. water in a given region) is inadmissible.  
Access to means of survival may not be used as an instrument of coercion.

**Art. 14.**  
Contracts, concessions, and claims to specific installations and extracted resources are recognized within the bounds of this Constitution and international law — not as title to lunar territory.

---

## Title V — Authority, Disputes, Responsibility

**Art. 15.**  
Until a representative lunar institution is established, recognized by persons present on the Moon and consistent with international law, disputes shall be resolved:
1. by mediation,
2. by agreed arbitration,
3. with respect for the jurisdiction of states responsible for the missions concerned, insofar as this does not violate Art. 3–7.

**Art. 16.**  
Responsibility for harm caused on the Moon lies with the entities that caused it — persons, operators, registering states — in accordance with the principles of space law and this Constitution.

**Art. 17.**  
Armed force on the Moon may serve only the defence of life and evacuation.  
Placement of weapons of mass destruction and offensive military action of a territorial character are prohibited.

---

## Title VI — Entry into Force, Amendment, Language

**Art. 18.**  
This text is an open draft.  
It acquires moral and practical force as it is voluntarily adopted by persons, bases, and organizations operating on the Moon — without prejudice to binding international obligations.

**Art. 19.**  
Amendments require open debate and broad agreement among the subjects that have adopted the Constitution.  
Title I and Art. 3 may not be amended so as to repeal the prohibition of sovereignty over the Moon or the dignity of the person.

**Art. 20.**  
The text is published in languages open to the community.  
Working version 0.1 was prepared in Polish; this is the English version of the same draft.
